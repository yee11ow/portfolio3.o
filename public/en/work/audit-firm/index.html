Concept study, self-initiated

Fifty years of experience the website was hiding

Audit and accountancy firm in Uppsala. Fourteen people, three authorised public accountants, in business since 1975.

Open the live concept →

The starting point

The firm holds an advantage no competitor can copy: fifty years of advising business owners in the region. The website showed almost none of it.

The site still ran on graphics and gif images from 2014, logo included. Meanwhile younger, digital-first firms appear beside them in search results with clear fixed-price messaging. An owner comparing firms online forms an impression in seconds, and that is where the firm was losing a lead it actually had.

Three things stood out

  • The history lived in the copy but not in the design. The anniversary was mentioned, but the visual language contradicted the message instead of carrying it. The home page celebrated fifty years while the services page said just over thirty.
  • No price signal anywhere. Predictable pricing is the competitors’ main selling point, and the site offered no answer to it.
  • The technique lagged behind the content. No H1 on the home page, every page marked lang="en-US" despite Swedish content, and a phone number you could not tap on a mobile.

What I changed, and why

  1. 01

    Built the home page around the firm’s own word.

    Their existing copy used a specific Swedish term for a trusted sounding board. A word the client chose themselves lands better than a generic headline about audit services, because it sounds like the firm rather than like a brochure.

  2. 02

    Turned the anniversary into an argument.

    Fifty years means the firm has walked clients through several economic cycles. That is what an owner wants to hear, so it was written as a benefit rather than a milestone. A number without meaning convinces nobody.

  3. 03

    Resolved the contradiction with a timeline.

    Fifty years in one place and thirty in another makes a reader stop trusting both. A single timeline makes the question impossible to ask.

  4. 04

    Redrew the logo as a vector.

    Same mark, same character, sharp on every screen instead of a 358 pixel gif. A blurry logo reads as carelessness, and carelessness is the last thing an accountant wants to signal.

  5. 05

    Added a clear statement on pricing.

    A fixed proposal before work begins. It neutralises the competitors’ strongest card without changing how the firm actually operates.

  6. 06

    Gave the team faces.

    A small firm sells on relationships. Name, role and photo for each person makes it concrete who you will be talking to, and that is often what decides the call.

  7. 07

    Mobile first the whole way through.

    Most people comparing firms do it on a phone, between two other things. If the number does not dial on one tap, the rest of the work is wasted.

How it would be measured

This is a concept study and was never launched, so there are no numbers to show and I am not going to invent any. In production I would track three: enquiries through the form per month, the share of visitors reaching the contact page, and the share of mobile visitors staying longer than twenty seconds.
